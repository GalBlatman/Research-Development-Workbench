# Research Idea Evaluation and Development Protocol

## Version 4: grounded questions, contribution-aware evaluation, and disciplined development

**Version date:** October 2, 2026  
**Replaces:** `Research_Idea_Rubric_v3.md`  
**Primary use:** Evaluate and develop theory-contributing empirical research in management and organizations.  
**Additional use:** Assess discovery, phenomenon-establishing, and testing projects without pretending that they already contain a new theoretical explanation.

**Status.** This is a source-informed decision protocol, not an empirically calibrated model of journal acceptance. It retains Version 3's ten dimensions, integer ratings, midpoint anchor, separate idea and study scores, 50/50 project summary, and prohibition on advance credit for repairs. The contribution routes, refinements to the criteria, readiness rules, and development procedures are protocol choices. The sources support the underlying questions, not the weights, cutoffs, or planning probabilities.

The central rule is:

> Judge the contribution the project actually promises. Require the argument and evidence appropriate to that contribution. Preserve the valuable insight while identifying the next piece of work that can change the decision.

This document is complete on its own. Version 3 and the preceding conversation are not required to apply it. References identify the supplied readings and the limits of their use. The theory-builder exports are treated as visible prompts, not as a recovered algorithm or evidence that the underlying book has been reviewed.

## 1. Decisions and quick use

The protocol answers two different questions: **How strong is the idea or specified project now?** and **What should be done next?** A strong current project and a worthwhile development action are not the same thing.

For an articulated explanatory contribution, retain these outputs:

| Output | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `I_raw`, 0–100 | Quality of the theoretical argument currently articulated, before considering the convenience of the available data. |
| `D_raw`, 0–100 | Adequacy of the specified study for the claim. Label it `PROPOSAL` or `COMPLETED STUDY`. |
| `P_raw`, 0–100 | Optional 50/50 summary of idea and study strength, subject to the hard stops and profile requirements. |
| `S0` | Current headline strength. State whether its base is `IDEA` or `PROJECT`. |
| `V(B)` | Expected strength after a specified next step within a common resource budget. This is a scenario index, not the full value of learning or a publication probability. |

For idea selection, use `S0 = I_raw`. For comparisons among sufficiently specified explanatory projects, use `S0 = P_raw`. Never mix those bases in one numerical ranking. When a required block has not been assessed, report it as `PENDING`, not zero or the midpoint.

For other contribution routes, use the route assessment in Section 2. Do not create a total by replacing a missing mechanism score with enthusiasm about discovery. A worthwhile discovery or replication can receive a positive development decision without an `I_raw` or `P_raw`.

### 1.1 Two depths of use

**Initial screen.** Use the short template in Section 16.1. Establish the question, stakes, intended contribution, strongest existing answer or unresolved observation, core insight to preserve, main uncertainty, and smallest useful next step. Do not claim to have completed all ten dimensions.

**Full evaluation.** Use the twelve-step procedure, the applicable audits, and the full card. Inspect the relevant project material and close predecessors. Record scores only for assessed elements. Complete numerical development scenarios only when their inputs are defensible.

Keep one record of each fact and refer back to it. The grounding check, dimension 1, and the evaluation card do not require three separate rewrites of the same paragraph. The protocol is a research worksheet, not an instruction to put every field into the manuscript.

### 1.2 Order of attention

Assess the intellectual task before allowing accessible data to determine its value. This is an **evaluation order**, not a rule that ideas must originate in theory. Questions may originate in observations, practice, theory, or an unexpected result. Iteration is legitimate; inventing a retrospective history of prediction is not. The Canvas explicitly allows iteration, and Hambrick makes the case for facts that precede explanations.[^canvas][^hambrick]

## 2. Contribution route, stage, and assessment status

### 2.1 Identify the intended contribution before applying its requirements

Use a primary route. A project may have supporting contributions, but they must not be used to evade the requirements of its central claim.

| Route | Main promise | Required assessment | Numerical treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EXPLAIN** | Develop, revise, integrate, clarify, or resolve uncertainty in an explanation of a consequential phenomenon. | Evaluate the theoretical change, because-logic, constructs, boundaries, and discriminating implications. | Use dimensions 1–7 and `I_raw` when the argument is sufficiently articulated. Add dimensions 8–10, `D_raw`, and `P_raw` when warranted. |
| **ESTABLISH** | Establish, characterize, document, or make sense of a consequential phenomenon whose explanation is not yet the delivered contribution. | Evaluate what will become known, what is already documented, the scope of the observation, protection against artifacts, and the inquiries or decisions it enables. | Report a route assessment. Use study ratings when specified, but do not manufacture `I_raw` or `P_raw`. |
| **TEST** | Test, replicate, or adjudicate an existing claim to resolve important uncertainty. | Specify the claim, its existing support, why uncertainty matters, what an informative test requires, and how different results would change confidence. | Report a route assessment. Use study ratings when specified. Do not require a new variable or theory to legitimate the test. |

These are V4 contribution categories, informed by Hambrick's argument that explanation, fact-finding, and testing can each advance knowledge.[^hambrick] They are not a taxonomy supplied by that paper.

An adjudication that genuinely changes the best explanation can qualify as EXPLAIN. The difference is its intellectual contribution, not whether its method is hypothesis testing. New terminology is not required. Equally, a replication does not become EXPLAIN simply because the introduction describes it as a theoretical extension.

**Discovery is a stage and approach, not automatically a fourth contribution type.** A discovery proposal may aim eventually to produce EXPLAIN while currently offering only a justified explanatory task. Record both. Until an account exists, use the discovery assessment below and leave the missing explanatory block pending. When an account emerges, assess the new version under EXPLAIN without treating the completed explanation as known at the proposal stage.

A synthesis, measure, or tool may support any route. When such an output is the entire contribution and does not fit these routes, identify the mismatch and give a tailored narrative assessment. Do not force unrelated work onto the ten-dimension total.

### 2.2 Assessing a route without an explanatory total

Use these questions for ESTABLISH, TEST, and pre-explanation discovery. Mark each `ADEQUATE FOR STAGE`, `DEVELOPMENT NEEDED`, `BLOCKING`, or `NOT INSPECTED`, with a reason. These are not points and are not summed.

| Assessment | ESTABLISH or pre-explanation discovery | TEST |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge need** | What consequential observation, experience, pattern, or uncertainty needs investigation? | Which claim matters enough that changing confidence in it would be valuable? |
| **Increment over existing knowledge** | What do prior accounts already establish, and what remains undocumented or poorly understood? | Why does this evidence add information rather than repeat an already settled demonstration? |
| **Scope and precision** | What population, cases, experiences, period, or process is being characterized? | What exact claim, conditions, outcome, and comparison are being tested? |
| **Capacity to learn** | How could the work reveal that the apparent phenomenon is absent, misdescribed, or differently structured? | What would supporting, conflicting, null, or imprecise evidence permit the researcher to conclude? |
| **Evidence strategy** | How will documentation, alternative interpretations, and possible selection or measurement artifacts be addressed? | Does the design fairly test the claim and separate a theory problem from a failed measurement or manipulation? |
| **Next use** | What subsequent question or decision becomes better founded? | What inquiry or decision no longer needs to rely on the same unresolved assumption? |

A **positive bounded-development decision** requires a consequential knowledge need, an identifiable increment, and a credible action that addresses the main uncertainty within the stated resources. Uninspected material makes the relevant endorsement provisional. A central unsupported assertion requires a premise check, not an endorsement of its truth.

A completed route contribution requires delivered evidence, not merely an attractive question. Apply the same honesty about scope, uncertainty, and alternatives as in EXPLAIN. Do not call a narrow description a causal explanation.

### 2.3 Record the stage separately

Record `EARLY IDEA`, `DISCOVERY PROPOSAL`, `SPECIFIED STUDY PROPOSAL`, or `COMPLETED STUDY`. Also identify the decision being made: initial screen, pilot investment, full-study commitment, or submission readiness.

A fully stated theoretical argument may be scored as an idea before evidence exists. A proposal study score assesses its specified design, not imagined findings. A completed study must be assessed against its actual evidence.

### 2.4 Distinguish evidence states

| State | Meaning and treatment |
|---|---|
| **Documented or verified** | The relevant source or check has been inspected and supports the stated, bounded claim. |
| **Specified but untested** | The argument or procedure is explicit. Assess its quality for the stage without crediting successful execution or favorable results. |
| **Contested** | Credible accounts disagree. Describe the disagreement; it may itself motivate the study. |
| **Missing** | A component required for this contribution and stage is absent. Treat it as a development gap. |
| **Not inspected** | The evaluator has not examined the necessary material. Withhold the affected judgment rather than infer a defect or adequacy. |
| **Contradicted** | Available evidence conflicts with the retained assertion. Revise or stop that claim. |
| **Not applicable** | The component is not required for this route. Explain why; do not redistribute its weight to inflate a total. |

**Absence of an answer is not evidence of a bad question. Absence of support is not permission to state the answer as fact.**

## 3. Scales and rating rules

### 3.1 Integer ratings, 0 to 10

The starting reference is **5: comparable to a solid, ordinary recent accepted paper on this dimension**. This is a benchmark convention to be improved through actual reference-paper scoring, not an empirical assertion that accepted papers average 5.

| Rating | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | The required element is absent, internally contradictory, or invalid as stated. Use only after assessment. |
| 1 | A label or aspiration exists, but not a usable argument or design component. |
| 2 | A major defect makes the element substantially inadequate. |
| 3 | A plausible beginning has a central unresolved weakness. |
| 4 | Near the accepted-paper reference, with a consequential weakness to resolve. |
| 5 | Solid accepted-paper-level treatment without a distinctive strength. |
| 6 | Clearly stronger than the ordinary reference in a specified respect. |
| 7 | A notable strength of a strong paper, supported by explicit reasoning or evidence. |
| 8 | Exceptional relative to relevant published benchmarks. |
| 9 | Rare and potentially field-shaping on this element, with a defensible comparison to outstanding work. |
| 10 | A decade-class exemplar on this element. Not perfection and not automatically a decade-class paper. |

Each rating needs a justification, its main limitation, an assessment status, and the material inspected. Use integers, not discretionary half-points or tenths. Record an adjacent plausible range when uncertainty matters. Aggregation may produce decimals; preserve them for calculation and display whole points. Evaluate all thresholds before rounding.

A score of 8 or above requires an inspected, relevant published comparison and an explicit dimension-level reason. Naming a famous paper is not a comparison. Compare the idea's stated reasoning or specified design, not hoped-for findings. When no appropriate comparison has been inspected, withhold the exceptional rating rather than invent one.

Do not force accepted papers or candidate ideas to a particular mean, variance, or distribution. A published paper can have an uneven profile. An accepted-paper anchor is not a universal requirement that each dimension exceed 5.

### 3.2 Keep the dimensions distinct

| Dimension | What it establishes, and does not establish |
|---|---|
| 1. Consequential puzzle | Why the answer matters, not why it is surprising. |
| 2. Interestingness | What the audience must reconsider, not whether the proposed explanation is genuinely new. |
| 3. Theoretical advance | What changes in the best explanation, not merely that a variable or setting is new. |
| 4. Explanatory logic | Why the account follows, not whether its empirical evidence is already available. |
| 5. Constructs, levels, timing | Whether the concepts and argument are precise, not whether the chosen variables measure them well. |
| 6. Discriminating implications | What observations would separate accounts, not whether the present sample contains those observations. |
| 7. Coherence, reach, usefulness | Whether the answer hangs together and can be reused, not the number of audiences listed. |
| 8. Construct–evidence alignment | Whether observations represent the intended concepts and distinction. |
| 9. Inferential credibility | Whether the specified comparisons and evidence warrant the inference. |
| 10. Setting and sampling | Whether selection of the setting and cases makes the required contrast visible. |

The same evidence may inform several dimensions, but the reason must differ. Do not repeat “novel and important” to justify five high scores. Likewise, one problem may genuinely affect more than one dimension; explain each effect rather than subtracting points repeatedly as a separate punishment.

### 3.3 Editorial anchors remain separate

| Target | Intended editorial meaning | Status in V4 |
|---:|---|---|
| 80 | Passes desk review | Uncalibrated target. |
| 90 | Probably one round of revise and resubmit | Uncalibrated target requiring comparable-stage manuscripts and revision histories. |
| 95 | A top-tier associate editor says, “I want this in my journal” | Uncalibrated advocacy target requiring informed editorial judgments. |
| 100 | Paper of the decade | Aspirational influence anchor, not something established by recent acceptances alone. |

Preserve these meanings. Until supported calibration exists, report the editorial field as `UNCALIBRATED`. Do not revive the earlier linear conversion. Raw 50 is the intended ordinary accepted-paper reference, not a failing grade or a 50% chance of acceptance. No source in this collection supplies a conversion from the raw scores to these outcomes.

## 4. The seven theory dimensions

Apply this numerical block to an articulated EXPLAIN contribution. Its relative weights sum to 75 and are normalized within `I_raw`; they do not give theory 75% of `P_raw`. Refer to the cross-check records rather than repeating them.

### 4.1 Dimension 1: Consequential puzzle | Weight 10

**Question:** What important misunderstanding or unresolved problem would remain without an answer?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Motivation rests on a literature gap, fashionable setting, or preferred theoretical style with no clear stake. |
| 5 | A recognized broad problem is linked to a precise question with a defensible scholarly or practical consequence. |
| 8 | Resolving the question would materially change an important diagnosis, expectation, or decision for a substantial audience. |
| 10 | The question enables progress on a consequential class of problems rather than only an isolated puzzle. |

**Required record:** Broad puzzle; narrow question; concrete instance or basis for the proposed question; claimed scope; scholarly audience; whose perspective defines the problem; why the answer is needed; premise status.

The importance of an industry alone earns no points. Immediate action is not the only consequence: better diagnosis, explanation, or accumulated knowledge can matter. State the particular contribution to a larger problem without pretending the study solves the entire problem. Grounding combines attention to the specific case and its broader relevance (Van de Ven, 2016); importance is not a substitute for rigor or a demand for a dramatic reversal (Tihanyi, 2020).[^grounding][^tihanyi]

### 4.2 Dimension 2: Audience-relative interestingness | Weight 10

**Question:** What would informed members of the named audience have to reconsider?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | The answer is obvious, irrelevant to the audience, or surprising only because its position has been distorted. |
| 5 | The proposal challenges, clarifies, or puts in tension a real expectation or unresolved dispute. |
| 8 | A compelling existing answer meets a credible, consequential alternative; the departure and any valid domain of the earlier answer are clear. |
| 10 | An important assumption or dispute is reorganized in a way that substantially changes understanding. |

**Required mystery test:** State the question without revealing the proposed answer. What would an informed reader expect, and how confidently? When the sign is predictable, what consequential uncertainty about mechanism, magnitude, conditions, sequence, or interpretation remains?

Verify the audience's expectation against actual work. “Everyone assumes” requires support. The compelling alternative is a substantive explanation, not automatically a statistical zero. Preserve where the earlier account remains right when a valid domain can be specified; do not invent one to complete a template.

Davis makes interestingness audience-relative and acknowledges that supporting a contested belief can be interesting. Colquitt and George supply the mystery test; Zuckerman supplies the compelling alternative and the valid domain of the earlier answer. Their use here does not require a sign reversal, concealment of information, or rhetorical surprise.[^davis][^topic][^zuckerman]

### 4.3 Dimension 3: Theoretical advance | Weight 15

**Question:** What changes in the best existing explanation?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | A familiar answer is applied elsewhere, relabeled, or extended with a variable without an identified intellectual change. |
| 5 | A specific distinction, boundary, elaboration, integration, or test makes a recognizable nontrivial addition. |
| 8 | The argument changes an important inference, reconciles findings, resolves a consequential disagreement, or enables a reusable explanation. |
| 10 | A substantial conversation can be reorganized around the insight; the advance is not merely a new name or combination. |

**Required record:** Closest predecessors; their actual explanation; before-and-after statement; type of contribution; inspected overlap; unresolved novelty questions; the argument's role in the broader body of theory.

Use the prior-work check in Section 6. A new setting or moderator may reveal a substantive boundary, but its inclusion alone is not the contribution. Familiar components can yield a new argument when their assembly changes what follows. Resolving a serious dispute can matter as much as overturning consensus. A direct test can qualify when the change in explanatory understanding is articulated; otherwise assess its value under TEST without demanding decorative novelty.[^hook][^hambrick]

### 4.4 Dimension 4: Explanatory logic and mechanism | Weight 15

**Question:** Why should the proposed process or relationship occur?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | A label, association, chronology, or restatement substitutes for explanation. |
| 5 | The relevant actors, actions, conditions, relations, and sequence are identified, with essential assumptions stated. |
| 8 | The explanation is coherent and economical and yields distinct implications beyond repeating the favored relationship. |
| 10 | A clear explanation makes previously puzzling patterns understandable together and yields useful implications beyond the immediate claim. |

**Required record:** Because-logic in plain language; necessary assumptions; process or relations that generate the outcome; conditions that activate or limit them; relevant sequence; consequences that follow.

Separate **the process**, **the circumstances under which it operates**, and **the observed events**. This distinction follows Van de Ven's discussion of Tsoukas; it does not imply that Tsoukas's original paper was independently reviewed here.[^practicaltheory] Use explanatory language appropriate to the research tradition rather than forcing every account into a mediator model.

For each essential step, state what would count as evidence and what remains an assumption. Assess empirical access under dimensions 8–10. Do not require direct observation of every step as a universal rule, but do not claim an unobserved step has been demonstrated. An indirect implication must actually discriminate the account.

A discovery plan is not an excellent mechanism. Leave an undeveloped explanatory block pending and use Section 2.2. A missing mechanism in a project that already claims to explain is a genuine weakness, not automatically an exemption.

### 4.5 Dimension 5: Constructs, levels, and timing | Weight 8

**Question:** Are the concepts distinct and is the argument consistent across units and time?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Neighboring concepts slide into each other, or levels and sequence conflict with the claim. |
| 5 | Central constructs and their boundaries, units, sequence, and cross-level links are explicit. |
| 8 | The distinctions remove a real ambiguity and sharpen the explanation without unsupported detail. |
| 10 | Conceptual clarification resolves a persistent confusion and enables more discriminating explanations. |

**Required record:** Concept definitions; nearest neighboring concepts; inclusion and exclusion conditions; units; timing; cross-level connections where relevant; distinction between concepts and variables.

For a portable concept, identify what stays the same across settings and what would make it no longer apply. Broadening a word until it covers everything is not generalization. When theories conflict, examine differences in level, timing, or conditions before declaring one false or introducing a new label.[^practicaltheory]

Specify a numerical theoretical effect range only when justified. Otherwise state an ordering, pattern, temporal sequence, or qualitative contrast. A smallest important effect for design purposes is not automatically a prediction supplied by theory.

### 4.6 Dimension 6: Discriminating implications and intellectual risk | Weight 9

**Question:** What observations would distinguish the account from serious alternatives and could genuinely weaken it?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Nearly any result can be accommodated, or the alternative is merely ceremonial. |
| 5 | A credible rival implies a different observation and the proposed explanation is exposed to an informative test. |
| 8 | A focused comparison can resolve an important ambiguity, including through evidence unfavorable to the preferred account. |
| 10 | Unusually discriminating observations could remove a persistent uncertainty in explanation. |

**Required record:** Strongest relevant alternatives; their actual predictions or interpretations; decisive contrast; evidence that would change the conclusion; necessary auxiliary assumptions.

There is no rival quota. One serious comparison is better than four implausible alternatives. Negative cases, sequences, interpretive contrasts, and quantitative patterns can each do the work. An observed sequence is not automatically a causal test. A nonsignificant coefficient is not automatically falsification. State what it could mean under the proposed measurement and uncertainty.

Distinguish an implication that is conceptually discriminating from a design that can observe it. The former is scored here; the latter belongs in the study block. The builder's visible prompts support linking competing predictions to discriminating evidence, but provide no hidden rule that a certain number of rivals earns a particular rating.[^builder]

### 4.7 Dimension 7: Coherence, reach, and usefulness | Weight 8

**Question:** Is there one contribution with enough scope to matter, limits that follow from its logic, and a concrete use beyond this presentation?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | The argument is too small for its question, a bundle of unrelated claims, or supported only by generic usefulness claims. |
| 5 | Supporting claims belong together, scope is warranted, and the answer changes inquiry, diagnosis, or action concretely. |
| 8 | A compact explanation can be reused beyond the immediate case while preserving its necessary conditions. |
| 10 | The insight provides a reusable way to understand a class of problems without claiming universal reach. |

**Required record:** One-sentence takeaway; sufficient scope; transfer conditions; a concrete downstream use; relevant entries in the usefulness profile.

Prefer the **smallest argument that fully answers the question**, not simply the shortest argument. One central contribution can require several connected hypotheses or complementary studies. This is the protocol's resolution of the tension between Zuckerman's focus and Colquitt and George's concern about undersampling the explanatory landscape.[^zuckerman][^topic]

Ask what a subsequent researcher could use without reconstructing the same distinction or argument, and what would still require new evidence. This adapts Patel and colleagues' idea of downstream reuse; it does not equate management theory with proof compression.[^patel]

Do not reward a longer list of stakeholders or literatures. Practical usefulness can be indirect or cumulative. A dissemination plan, media attention, or citation count is not itself a theoretical advance. Use Section 10 to keep those claims separate.

## 5. The three study dimensions

The relative weights sum to 25 and are normalized within `D_raw`. These questions apply to the claim actually made, including ESTABLISH and TEST. The rating remains route- and stage-labeled. It is not a substitute for assessing the value of the research question.

### 5.1 Dimension 8: Construct–evidence alignment | Weight 10

**Question:** Do the observations represent the concepts and the distinction the claim requires?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Convenient variables are relabeled as the required concepts, or an indispensable distinction is invisible. |
| 5 | Defensible observations address the main concepts, with validation evidence or a stage-appropriate validation plan. |
| 8 | Direct or complementary evidence separates neighboring concepts and addresses the crucial distinction with unusual clarity. |
| 10 | The evidence makes the central distinction exceptionally clear with little dependence on ambiguous proxies. |

**Required record:** Concept-to-observation map; timing; validation evidence or plan; source provenance; what is unobserved; what must be inferred.

Using an existing measure is not automatically a weakness. Changing what it is claimed to measure without supporting evidence is. Do not treat measures with the same label as identical or differently named measures as necessarily distinct. These concerns follow Bono and McNamara.[^design]

EXPLAIN must address the support needed for its explanatory claim. ESTABLISH need not observe an unpromised mechanism; it must measure or document its phenomenon adequately. TEST must represent the original claim fairly, including essential conditions and differences introduced by adaptation.

### 5.2 Dimension 9: Credibility for the claimed inference | Weight 10

**Question:** Which observations supply the inference, and do they justify this kind of claim?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | The central inference is unsupported, serious alternatives are unaddressed, or apparent sample size hides inadequate information. |
| 5 | Comparisons, timing, uncertainty, and alternatives fit the claim; support is examined at the actual analytic level. |
| 8 | The most important alternatives are addressed, and the inference is not an artifact of a few observations, an unsupported range, or fragile measurement. |
| 10 | Especially discriminating or complementary evidence makes the bounded conclusion unusually credible. |

**Required record:** The support audit in Section 7, matched to the method and stage. A method name does not complete this record.

Do not assign automatic points to fixed effects, natural experiments, qualitative work, large samples, or multiple studies. Do not award points for statistical significance. A study can be excellent because it rules out a consequential effect or changes confidence in an accepted claim. A narrow warranted conclusion is preferable to an unsupported broad one, but its narrower intellectual contribution must be assessed separately.

### 5.3 Dimension 10: Informative setting and sampling | Weight 5

**Question:** Does the setting and sampling logic reveal what the research needs to learn?

| Rating | Dimension anchor |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Convenience determines the sample without a defensible connection to the question. |
| 5 | Required variation, contrasts, or process evidence can be observed in a justified sample, with access status stated. |
| 8 | The setting offers unusually informative contrasts or access, with appropriate inclusion logic and transfer limits. |
| 10 | The setting makes a crucial distinction visible that would otherwise be difficult to investigate. |

**Required record:** Why this setting; unit and sampling rule; necessary contrasts; coverage and selection; missingness; access status; credible alternatives.

Rare settings are not automatically better. A large collection of cases is not better if the needed comparison is absent. Verified access affects feasibility, but must not inflate the idea score. Sampling quality and the actual information supplied are related but distinct judgments under dimensions 10 and 9.

## 6. Grounding, prior-work, and contribution-promise checks

These checks make the existing dimensions easier to apply. They do not add points or create extra dimensions.

### 6.1 Ground the phenomenon and the knowledge need

Record a concrete case or basis for the question, the evidence supporting the claimed scope, whose perspective defines the issue, and what specifically needs to be learned. Identify how the particular case relates to a broader problem, without requiring every valuable episode to be a population-wide regularity.

Distinguish the **scholarly audience** from the **people whose experience defines the phenomenon** and the **people who may be affected by use of the findings**. These groups may overlap, but they need not. State the researcher's perspective and important alternative framings. Engagement informs the diagnosis; stakeholder approval does not establish truth or determine scientific worth.

The sources of grounding can include observations, credible records, prior studies, and accounts from people who experience the issue. An illustrative hypothetical is permitted if labeled as such; it cannot serve as evidence that a phenomenon exists. For a theory-led question, explain what makes the proposed relationship worth investigating without claiming that it has already been observed.

Where an initial question is too broad, map the dependency: what must be established first, what this study can answer, and what subsequent inquiry it makes possible. This applies Van de Ven's discussion of grounded diagnosis and cumulative questions.[^grounding]

### 6.2 Check the closest prior work, not just familiar labels

Identify the closest explanations, observations, or tests relevant to the promised contribution. Inspect the work needed to justify the novelty assessment, not only the references the proposal happens to cite. Record what was searched or supplied, what was actually read, and what remains unchecked.

Use these descriptive categories; do not convert them to another numerical score:

| Relationship to prior work | What must be established |
|---|---|
| **Already stated** | Identify the matching claim, mechanism, and scope in an inspected source. |
| **Immediate application** | Explain how the existing account yields the proposed conclusion without a substantive new step. |
| **Nontrivial assembly** | Identify the familiar ingredients and the new relation, assumption, or integration needed to produce the conclusion. |
| **Distinct explanatory change** | State the departure and what the earlier account cannot explain or distinguish. |
| **Unresolved overlap** | Identify the missing inspection or uncertainty. Lack of a recognized precedent does not establish novelty. |

Ballinger and Johnson support inspecting central work and disclosing the limits of expertise. The distinction between an existing equivalent, an immediate specialization, and nontrivial assembly is also an adaptation of Patel and colleagues' containment assessment.[^firstreview][^patel] Management literature is not a closed, machine-verified library: this check cannot certify exhaustive novelty.

A matched prediction alone does not prove theoretical duplication if the mechanism or scope differs substantively. Conversely, differently named constructs do not establish a new mechanism. For ESTABLISH, evaluate overlap in what is documented. For TEST, an existing claim is the object of study, not a novelty failure; the question is what important uncertainty the new test resolves.

### 6.3 Check that the contribution promise is deliverable

Write a short entry statement using Grant and Pollock's three questions: Who cares? What is known, what remains unresolved, and why does it matter? What will be learned?[^hook]

Then align the following in a single record:

| Element | Required statement |
|---|---|
| Broad puzzle | The larger problem the work addresses. |
| Narrow question | The question this project can answer. |
| Contribution claim | The change in explanation, established knowledge, or confidence that would result. |
| Required argument and evidence | What must be true, observed, or distinguished for that contribution to follow. |
| Actual delivery | What the articulated argument and specified design can deliver, or what a completed study actually establishes. |

A broad puzzle may motivate a narrower contribution. The narrow result must do the work claimed for it. Distinguish an empirical association, a causal effect, an explanation of the effect, and a recommendation for intervention. Establishing one does not automatically establish the others.

When the promise is too broad, narrow it and rescore the resulting intellectual contribution, or specify additional evidence. Do not simply soften a few verbs while retaining the same unsupported inference. A mismatch that concerns only presentation requires clarification; a mismatch that defeats the central contribution invokes Section 8.

Writing quality affects assessability. If an argument is clear but inelegant, do not reduce its intellectual score for taste. If it cannot be understood, identify what is unclear rather than inventing a better argument. A polished hook earns no separate points.

## 7. Inference-support audit

Complete this audit for each principal claim at the full-evaluation stage. It is inherited and refined from Version 3, not a formula supplied by the source essays. Adapt the diagnostics to the design; retain the underlying question: **what evidence bears the inferential burden?**

### 7.1 Quantitative studies

| Check | Required record |
|---|---|
| **Analytic sample** | Exact sample, observation unit, inclusion rules, missingness, and exclusions. |
| **Independent changes or assignments** | Level at which the proposed cause varies; independent policies, shocks, assignments, or clusters where relevant. |
| **Usable comparison** | Which observations supply variation under the actual sample, fixed effects, regressors, and weights. |
| **Residual spread and overlap** | Focal-predictor variation and common support. For an interaction, assess it after accounting for main terms and other regressors. |
| **Concentration and influence** | Top-unit and top-three-unit shares where appropriate, a justified concentration diagnostic, and deletion or influence checks where feasible. |
| **Outcome information** | Events, minority outcomes, transitions, risk sets, dispersion, or other information relevant to this model, and their distribution across units. |
| **Substantive sensitivity** | The smallest consequential effect or a defensible range, expressed on an interpretable scale. |
| **Design sensitivity** | What effects the proposed inference and dependence structure could distinguish, with its assumptions. Do not invent power or detectable effects. |
| **Bounds** | A theoretical or physical bound only when independently justified. An overall mean does not by itself impose a coefficient ceiling. |
| **Remaining uncertainty** | What has not been tested, whether consequential effects could be missed, and which alternative interpretations remain. |

For a linear model with nonnegative weights, the inherited focal-predictor concentration diagnostic is:

```text
s_g = sum_{i in g}(w_i * x_tilde_i^2) / sum_i(w_i * x_tilde_i^2)
G_eff = 1 / sum_g(s_g^2)
```

Here `x_tilde` is the focal predictor residualized against the other regressors on the exact analytic sample using the model's weights. Use `w_i = 1` for an unweighted model. When residual variation is zero, report `NO IDENTIFYING VARIATION`, not a fabricated count.

`G_eff` describes concentration of usable predictor variation. It is not a count of independent clusters, a degrees-of-freedom formula, a sufficient power calculation, or proof of causal identification. Nonlinear models may require different information or influence diagnostics. Explain the choice. There is no universal cutoff and no automatic transfer of a threshold from a previous project.

For descriptive work with no focal predictor, record the relevant sampling, coverage, distributional, and precision evidence instead of forcing this calculation. For testing, separate a precisely estimated negligible result from an imprecise result that remains compatible with consequential effects. In every case, the inference must match the observation and design.

### 7.2 Qualitative and process studies

Identify the cases, episodes, actors, records, and observation periods supporting each central inference. Distinguish independent sources from repetitions of one account. Identify contrasts, negative cases, alternative interpretations, and absent variation. State which processes were observed directly and which were inferred, including reliance on retrospective reports or unusually influential episodes.

A small number of cases can support an excellent focused process account. A single episode is not inherently disqualifying. The question is whether the observations support the scope and type of inference claimed, not whether a case quota is met. For discovery, document how interpretations evolved and how contrary material affected the account.

### 7.3 Before evidence exists

For each relevant audit item, use `EXPECTED`, `VERIFIED`, `PENDING`, or `UNAVAILABLE`. These describe check status, not a rating of the hypothesis. State the proposed check, its data source, and whether failure would require a different claim or design.

A specified design can be assessed as a proposal while empirical checks remain pending. Distinguish this from a proposal too vague to assess. Pending checks prevent claims of demonstrated support and may prevent full-study commitment when they concern an indispensable comparison. They do not automatically imply that the design is poor.

### 7.4 Source and analysis integrity

Maintain a trace from each claim to its observations and analysis. Distinguish predictions made before examining outcomes from exploratory findings and subsequent explanations. Disclose material sample and specification decisions. Iteration can improve research, but discovering a result does not turn it into an earlier prediction. These are protocol safeguards consistent with the concern about contrived surprise and post hoc causal claims in Tihanyi and the design concerns in Bono and McNamara.[^tihanyi][^design]

Do not equate “verified” with “causally established.” Specify what was verified: a source exists, a variable is available, a comparison is present, or an analysis supports a bounded conclusion.

## 8. Calculations, hard stops, and quality profiles

### 8.1 Calculate only assessed blocks

Let each applicable `d_i` be an integer from 0 to 10. For EXPLAIN:

```text
I_uncapped = (10/75) * (10*d1 + 10*d2 + 15*d3 + 15*d4 + 8*d5 + 9*d6 + 8*d7)
D_raw      = (10/25) * (10*d8 + 10*d9 + 5*d10)
P_uncapped = 0.50*I_uncapped + 0.50*D_raw
```

Apply all relevant idea caps using the lowest applicable cap:

```text
I_raw = min(I_uncapped, applicable idea caps)
P_before_project_caps = 0.50*I_raw + 0.50*D_raw
P_raw = min(P_before_project_caps, applicable project caps)
```

When no cap applies, retain the original value. Current V4 specifies no additional separate cap on `D_raw`; study defects are reflected in its ratings and in project hard stops. Display the uncapped and reported values when they differ, with the reasons. Do not subtract a cap as a penalty or apply repeated deductions for the same rule.

If all dimensions equal 5, all three uncapped scores equal 50. If all equal 8, all equal 80. The 50/50 combination is a project-balance convention, not an acceptance model. Retain integer profiles and unrounded totals. Round displayed raw totals to the nearest whole point, with exact halves upward. Apply gates to unrounded values.

ESTABLISH and TEST may receive `D_raw` using the study block when it is assessable, with the route attached. Their `I_raw` and `P_raw` fields read `NOT APPLICABLE TO THIS ROUTE`, not zero. A discovery project awaiting an eventual explanatory account uses `PENDING` for that account. Do not rank scientific value by study score alone.

### 8.2 Premise rule

A question about **whether** a pattern exists does not require its answer in advance. A disputed phenomenon can be the subject of inquiry. A particular episode need not represent a population unless the claim says it does. An explanation that asserts the phenomenon as fact does require an appropriate basis.

If a central asserted phenomenon has neither supporting evidence nor a credible establishing plan, dimension 1 is at most 3 and `I_raw` is capped at 39 for EXPLAIN. The project cannot receive a strong-idea endorsement. Its next permissible investment is premise verification or an explicit reformulation of the question.

If a credible establishing plan exists but the asserted premise remains unsupported, label the explanation `PREMISE-CONDITIONAL`. Its articulated logic may be assessed conditionally, but it cannot receive an unqualified strong or exceptional endorsement or justify full-study commitment before the indispensable check. A bounded study designed to establish the premise can proceed.

If evidence contradicts the retained premise, mark the explanatory version `BLOCKED AS STATED`. Do not continue recommending it as an established explanation. A revised claim, a narrower version, or a study of why the belief persists must be identified as a new version, not treated as a silent repair.

This adapts Van de Ven's discussion of establishing the phenomenon while preserving the stage distinctions above. Merton is used through Van de Ven, not claimed as an independently inspected source.[^grounding]

### 8.3 Other hard stops

| Problem | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Audience or research question cannot be identified | Withhold the final intellectual assessment until specified. An early clarifying action remains possible. |
| EXPLAIN offers no nontrivial advance beyond applying an existing answer | Cap `I_raw` at 39. Evaluate an articulated TEST or ESTABLISH version on its own merits rather than declaring all such work worthless. |
| Surprise without any consequential scholarly, organizational, or societal stake | Cap `I_raw` at 69; no strong or exceptional idea label. A purely scholarly stake can qualify if concrete and consequential. |
| Central question–design mismatch | Cap `P_raw` at 39 and block submission readiness. Retain the separately assessed idea score. |
| Evidence cannot support the central kind of inference promised | Cap `P_raw` at 39 until the claim or design changes. |
| Central contribution is broader than the argument and evidence can deliver | Apply the appropriate idea or project rule above. Narrow and rescore, or redesign. Do not add an extra arbitrary penalty. |
| Required dimensions have not been inspected | Withhold the affected block total; no midpoint substitution. |

A hard stop concerns the present claim or the proposed commitment. It need not mean permanent abandonment of the topic. Specify exactly what would remove it and what decision remains possible in the meantime.

### 8.4 Strong and exceptional explanatory ideas

**Strong idea:** `I_raw >= 70`; dimensions 3 and 4 each at least 7; dimensions 1 and 6 each at least 6; every remaining theory dimension at least 4; no unresolved blocking premise or unidentified audience.

**Exceptional idea:** `I_raw >= 80`; dimensions 3 and 4 each at least 8; dimensions 1 and 2 each at least 7; every remaining theory dimension at least 6; relevant close predecessors and required high-score benchmarks inspected; no unresolved central premise condition.

Failure to earn an excellence label is not a fatal flaw. A 6 is already above the ordinary reference on the stated scale. An uneven but worthwhile idea may deserve development without satisfying the strong profile. Do not use these profiles as universal minimum standards for valuable science or for all contribution routes.

When a justified rating range crosses a gate, report the classification as borderline or provisional and identify the disputed judgment. A rounded 70 does not pass a threshold when the unrounded total is below 70.

### 8.5 Readiness and project endorsements

**Proposal readiness for the next stage:** The intended contribution and study are sufficiently specified; indispensable prerequisites for the proposed commitment are established or included in the bounded pilot itself; no unresolved central mismatch defeats that stage. This is not submission readiness.

**Basic submission readiness for an EXPLAIN empirical manuscript:** A completed study; `I_raw >= 50`; dimensions 3 and 4 each at least 5; a defensible consequential stake; dimensions 8 and 9 each at least 5; no remaining idea-side or study-side hard stop; no indispensable support check still unverified; and an aligned contribution promise. The explicit idea-side floors are new V4 conventions, not editorial estimates.

**Strong project:** A strong explanatory idea; `D_raw >= 60`; dimensions 8 and 9 each at least 6; and no blocking mismatch or indispensable unresolved prerequisite for the claimed stage.

**Exceptional project:** An exceptional explanatory idea; `D_raw >= 70`; dimensions 8 and 9 each at least 7; and the same stage-appropriate prerequisites.

Attach `PROPOSED` or `COMPLETED` to project endorsements. A high proposal score describes a strong specified design, not demonstrated findings. An excellent idea does not repair inadequate evidence, and excellent evidence does not create a theoretical advance.

For ESTABLISH and TEST, submission readiness requires an adequate completed route assessment, dimensions 8 and 9 each at least 5, delivered support for the central claim, an honest contribution promise, and no hard stop. Do not impose EXPLAIN's mechanism or theoretical-novelty floors.

**Publication fit remains a separate field.** Record the intended audience and article type, supporting material inspected, and any unresolved fit issue. A scientifically ready study is not automatically suitable for every outlet. Historical editorials and normative essays in this collection do not certify current journal policy or acceptance odds.

## 9. The twelve-step procedure

### Intellectual pass

1. **Identify route, stage, and decision.** Name the contribution promised, audience, target article type, material available, and what commitment is being considered.
2. **Ground the question.** State the particular case or basis, claimed scope, stakeholder perspectives, knowledge need, and premise status.
3. **Build the strongest existing answer.** Describe why it is reasonable, where it holds when known, and the genuine unresolved issue. Complete the mystery test.
4. **Locate the contribution.** Inspect close predecessors. State the theoretical change, newly established knowledge, or important change in confidence. Record uncertain overlap.
5. **Define concepts, units, timing, and scope.** Separate concepts from available variables. Identify what stays constant across applications.
6. **Develop the explanation or explanatory task.** For EXPLAIN, state the because-logic and assumptions. For discovery, ESTABLISH, or TEST, do not invent an unpromised mechanism.
7. **Specify informative contrary evidence.** Identify serious alternatives, possible artifacts, and the observations that could change the conclusion.
8. **Assess current intellectual strength.** Record the core insight to preserve, concrete downstream use, and strongest rejection argument. Score `I_raw` only where applicable and sufficiently assessed; otherwise finish the route assessment.

### Evidence and development pass

9. **Specify the evidence needed.** Start with the claim and required contrast, then compare possible settings, measures, and designs. Do not let an available dataset silently rewrite the question.
10. **Evaluate the specified study and promised contribution.** Complete the applicable support audit, score dimensions 8–10 when warranted, compute eligible totals, and run the promise check.
11. **Specify the next bounded action.** Identify the one or two weaknesses that drive the decision; choose a repair or decisive check; define its deliverable, resource budget, outcome branches, and any comparable score scenarios.
12. **Apply gates and make the decision.** Report current strength, expected post-step strength where justified, remaining uncertainty, scientific value, target fit, and the next action. Record version changes and assessment limitations. For a set of ideas, run Section 13 before ranking.

This is a synthesis of the source questions and the builder's visible sequence, not an algorithm recovered from its empty exports. Iteration is allowed. A newly observed problem may require returning to an earlier step. Preserve the history of what changed and why.[^builder][^canvas]

## 10. Usefulness and impact profile

This is a compact supporting record for dimensions 1 and 7 and for development planning. It is **not five additional scoring dimensions**. Complete the forms relevant to the project; one concrete use is better than five generic claims.

### 10.1 Select the relevant forms

Wickert and colleagues distinguish scholarly, practical, societal, policy, and educational impact. V4 uses those forms as prompts, not mandatory targets for every paper.[^impact]

| Form | A concrete question |
|---|---|
| **Scholarly** | What explanation, distinction, empirical basis, or test will subsequent researchers no longer need to reconstruct? |
| **Practical** | Who could diagnose, decide, or act differently, in which recognizable situation? |
| **Societal** | What problem affecting people or communities becomes better understood, visible, or addressable? |
| **Policy** | What public or organizational decision could the evidence inform, under which goals and constraints? |
| **Educational** | What would students understand, diagnose, debate, or investigate differently? |

For each selected form record: **audience; proposed change; route to use; evidence status; implementation or transfer conditions; main risks and affected parties; next useful step**. The audience need not be corporate managers. Specify whose benefit is being considered.

### 10.2 Keep different claims separate

| Claim | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| **Potential relevance** | A reason the knowledge could matter to a specified audience. |
| **Route to use** | A credible way to connect the knowledge to a use, including the necessary form, relationship, or intermediary. |
| **Attention or reach** | Evidence the work was encountered or discussed. This does not establish uptake or benefit. |
| **Use** | Evidence the work informed understanding, a decision, a practice, teaching, or further inquiry. |
| **Consequences of use** | Evidence of the resulting change, with attribution and uncertainty appropriate to the claim. |

These distinctions are V4's operational adaptation of the impact readings. Aguinis and colleagues examine citations and web visibility among highly cited scholars; that does not validate an idea-level measure of societal benefit. Bansal and colleagues distinguish producing knowledge from translating and using it; Wickert and colleagues broaden the forms of influence.[^scholarlyimpact][^bridge][^impact]

At proposal stage, most claims concern potential and a proposed route. Do not call them demonstrated impact. At completed-study stage, publication alone still does not establish external use or beneficial consequences. Do not penalize a theoretical contribution simply because realized impact takes time to become observable.

### 10.3 Make use plausible without surrendering independence

When practical use is central to the claim, understand the intended users' problem and the knowledge they already use. Record the basis for that understanding. Early dialogue may sharpen the question. Credible records or other appropriate evidence may also inform it. Do not require a practitioner partner for every project or allow lack of immediate user enthusiasm to negate scientific value.

Specify whether use needs a synthesis, a diagnostic tool, a decision aid, a case, a discussion with users, or an intermediary. Include the resources needed for that work in development planning. Do not demand that one article simultaneously function as a journal paper, policy manual, and classroom exercise. Bansal and colleagues' account supports separate translation work while retaining critical distance.[^bridge]

Keep empirically supported findings, theory-derived implications, and proposed practices distinct. Simplify communication without erasing a condition essential to the conclusion. Stakeholder requests that change the question, scope, or evidential standard require an explicit decision, not silent expansion.

### 10.4 Recommendations and responsibility

For a proposed intervention, identify who could benefit, who could bear costs, the assumptions connecting evidence to the recommendation, and what has not been tested. Reporting an uncomfortable finding is not a defect. Claiming that any use of it will necessarily be beneficial is.

For an actual policy recommendation, use Wickert and colleagues' **advocacy, control, and ethics** prompts: the position or goal being advocated; the instrument and context through which it could operate; and the values, competing consequences, and ethical concerns involved. Do not require advocacy when the project is descriptive or explanatory rather than prescriptive.[^impact]

For educational use, identify whether the research changes content, develops research skills, supports discussion, or enables student inquiry. A planned teaching case is a route to educational use, not proof that learning improved.

## 11. Developmental evaluation and repair

### 11.1 The evaluator's obligations

Read the complete material supplied for the relevant stage before finalizing objections. Do not infer that an issue is absent because it was not in the first section. Disclose the scope of inspection and relevant limits of expertise. Identify both the strongest defensible insight and the objections that actually determine the decision.

Use Lepak's developmental orientation and Ballinger and Johnson's respect, reasons, recommendations, and recognition as standards for the **assessment**, not as points awarded to the idea.[^reviewing][^firstreview] Recognizing potential is not advance repair credit. Honest negative conclusions are permitted and sometimes necessary.

**Core insight to preserve:** State the most valuable insight present, why it matters, and what a revision must retain. If no distinctive insight can yet be defended, say so. Do not manufacture praise to balance criticism.

**Decision-driving objection:** Identify the exact claim, the problem with it, why the problem matters, the source or reasoning supporting the criticism, and an appropriate response. If no feasible repair is identified within the available resources, state that directly.

Distinguish three severities: **blocking** defeats the central claim or intended commitment; **substantive** materially limits value or credibility but may be repairable; **minor** improves communication or implementation without deciding the project's worth. Prioritize the one or two central issues. Keep any remaining necessary checks in a secondary record, not a competing list of urgent demands.

### 11.2 Improve this idea, not the evaluator's preferred paper

Recommend the smallest change that resolves the substantive problem while preserving the insight. A different research question or explanation is a **replacement or pivot**, not a repair already achieved. Record it as a separate version and assess what has been gained and lost.

Do not reopen settled wording or add preemptive limitations unless the choice changes the claim, its support, or the reader's understanding. Do not require allegiance to a favored theoretical tradition. A well-reasoned alternative repair can address an objection even if it differs from the evaluator's suggestion.

### 11.3 Repair menu

| Diagnosed problem | Possible next action | What counts as success |
|---|---|---|
| Unsupported premise | Inspect primary records, conduct a bounded descriptive check, or reformulate as a question. | The status and scope of the premise become clear enough to decide what research is warranted. |
| Ambiguous novelty | Read the nearest predecessors and write the actual overlap and departure. | The remaining contribution is identifiable and not merely relabeled. |
| Restatement instead of explanation | Specify actors, actions, constraints, relations, or sequence; remove circular steps. | The conclusion follows from an articulated account with informative implications. |
| Apparently conflicting theories | Examine whether different levels, periods, or conditions explain the conflict; retain a genuine tension where warranted. | The relationship between accounts becomes clearer without inventing a distinction solely to avoid contrary evidence. |
| Stretched or overlapping concepts | Define exclusions, split conflated concepts, or narrow their scope. | The intended distinction becomes usable across the stated applications. |
| Weak measure | Validate, replace, or add a complementary observation. | The evidence better represents the concept and separates its nearest neighbor. |
| Inadequate inference | Inspect the usable comparison; revise design, add discriminating evidence, or narrow the claim. | The resulting claim is supportable, not merely statistically significant. |
| Overbroad promise | Align the question, argument, evidence, and contribution statement. | The contribution actually follows; the revised scope is rescored. |
| Unclear practical use | Examine the user's problem, create an appropriate translation, or work with an intermediary. | A credible use becomes specified or tested without overstating its evidence. |
| Unfocused project | Remove unrelated claims or combine parts that serve the same central argument. | Enough scope remains to answer the question without multiplying separate contributions. |

The timing, level, and conceptual options draw on Van de Ven's forum overview. The practical-use options draw on Bansal and colleagues. The table is a V4 repair aid, not a source-derived mapping from a tactic to a numerical gain.[^practicaltheory][^bridge]

### 11.4 Required repair record

Record the weakness and affected dimensions; evidence; severity; insight to preserve; exact deliverable and success condition; material already inspected; remaining access and substantive uncertainties; resource requirement; outcome branches; gates that would lift; and whether the action improves the argument, the study, or only the route to use.

Include numerical scenarios only under Section 12's conditions. A measurement repair does not automatically create theoretical novelty. Better framing earns an intellectual change only if the revised claim is actually articulated and stronger. A proposed repair is not complete until the relevant work has been inspected.

## 12. Choosing the next investment

### 12.1 Define the action and common resource budget

For each comparison, specify a common budget `B`: researcher time and, where relevant, direct cost, assistance, access, and calendar constraints. Record the resources each action actually uses, not just the common ceiling.

Every action needs a deliverable. “Improve theory” is not a deliverable; “write and check a revised argument separating the two proposed processes” can be. Divide a large reconstruction into a useful milestone within `B`, or compare it under another budget. Do not compare a full study to a short clarification as though their commitments were identical.

When no budget is specified, report useful actions and scenarios but withhold a numerical investment ranking. When small actions leave resources unused, make that visible. A combination or sequence of actions must fit the same budget before it can be compared with a larger commitment.

### 12.2 Give decisive checks their own decision priority

Before comparing expected score gains, ask whether a bounded check could reveal that the main planned investment rests on a false premise, unavailable contrast, or invalid measure. Mark an action `DECISIVE_CHECK` when a plausible result would materially change whether or how the larger commitment proceeds.

Record the outcomes and the response to each: proceed, narrow, redesign, redirect, or stop. Include an inconclusive or access-failure outcome when realistic. Name the fallback and resources that would remain. If the fallback is not specified, do not credit it as a valuable completed alternative.

A check that prevents wasted work can be worthwhile even if it lowers the project's expected strength. Learning that an argument is wrong does not damage science merely because its score falls. Do not veto an indispensable check because its expected score gain is negative. Conversely, do not call every small task valuable information: identify which decision it could change.

This decision priority extends Version 3's decisive-check safeguard. It is not a formally estimated value-of-information model and supplies no arbitrary information bonus.

### 12.3 Expected strength after the next step

For a comparable, scoreable explanatory project or idea, define:

| Input | Definition |
|---|---|
| `S0` | Current reported strength on a declared `IDEA` or `PROJECT` base. |
| `S_plus` | Conditional strength of the explicitly described version if the named repair succeeds within `B`. |
| `S_minus` | Strength of the explicitly described version if the repair does not succeed or reveals a weakness. |
| `p` | Planning probability of reaching the specified repair-success state within `B`. |

Then:

```text
V_a(B) = p*S_plus + (1-p)*S_minus
Expected score change = V_a(B) - S0
```

When failure leaves the project unchanged, and only then:

```text
V_a(B) = S0 + p*(S_plus-S0)
```

For several distinct outcomes:

```text
V_a(B) = sum_k(p_k*S_k), with sum_k(p_k) = 1
```

Use the same route, score base, and relevant stage of claim across the versions being compared. Apply the same scoring rules and caps to every numerical branch. Name any narrowed or rewritten claim sufficiently to assess it. Do not mix an idea score in one branch with a project score in another. A transition to a different contribution route requires a separate route assessment, not a made-up common score.

If the original version stops and no viable same-base branch has been described, record `STOP`; do not silently replace missing research value with zero or fabricate a rescue project just to calculate an expectation. Use the decision branches without `V(B)` when they are not numerically comparable. ESTABLISH, TEST, and incomplete discovery evaluations can support a development decision without this index.

Show the no-change option separately. Among comparable feasible actions, identify the highest expected post-step strength, the expected change, and the resource requirement. Do not rank by gain alone: a weak idea can improve greatly and still remain weaker than an alternative. Do not mechanically select the largest `V(B)` across different gates, commitments, routes, or indispensable checks.

The formula uses approximate interval-valued judgments and subjective planning probabilities. It does not estimate acceptance, realized impact, total research value, or career utility. Its V3 label, “development value,” is replaced by **expected strength after the next step** to keep that boundary explicit.

### 12.4 Separate completing the work from repairing the problem

Two questions need different answers: can the action be completed within `B`, and will the resulting evidence or argument resolve the diagnosed weakness? Data access can make the first likely while leaving the second uncertain. A completed study with an unfavorable result may be informative without rescuing the original explanation.

Where probabilities are useful and defensible, distinguish completion, information obtained, and the substantive branch. If probabilities are combined, use explicit conditional probabilities rather than treating dependent events as independent. No favorable coefficient is a repair-success condition.

V3's optional planning priors are retained only as starting scenarios, not estimates:

| Repair situation | Optional starting `p` | Sensitivity range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bounded repair; relevant material and technical route inspected; limited substantive uncertainty | 0.80 | 0.60–0.95 |
| Credible route with a material unverified access, measurement, or compatibility dependency | 0.50 | 0.25–0.75 |
| Speculative repair or a substantial unverified dependency | 0.20 | 0.00–0.40 |
| No feasible repair within the stated budget | 0.00 | 0.00 |

Do not use a default where it misrepresents the risk. A premise does not receive `p = 0.80` merely because the required data are accessible. State the reason for both `p` and the conditional score. When neither can be defended, use qualitative scenarios or a break-even calculation rather than inventing precision.

### 12.5 Break-even and sensitivity

For an action compared with an alternative value `V_alt`, when `S_plus > S_minus`:

```text
p_break_even = (V_alt-S_minus) / (S_plus-S_minus)
```

Values below 0 or above 1 indicate dominance under the stated branch values. If the two branch scores are equal, probability does not affect the comparison. Do not report a meaningless divided-by-zero threshold.

Vary the uncertain ratings, conditional gains, probabilities, and resource assumptions that could change the choice. Label resulting ranges as planning sensitivity, not statistical confidence intervals. When reasonable assumptions reverse the ranking, report an unresolved comparison and identify the smallest observation or clarification that could distinguish the choices.

### 12.6 What a useful action can accomplish

An action may strengthen an argument, make evidence more informative, establish a premise, expose an error, narrow a defensible claim, enable a later question, or improve the route to use. These are different achievements. Record the actual one. Translation can improve practical use without increasing `I_raw`; a decisive unfavorable test can justify stopping without counting as failed research; and a new explanation must be assessed as written rather than assumed to exist after a successful pilot.

## 13. Comparing a set of ideas

### 13.1 Group substantive duplicates

Before ranking, group candidates by their central question, explanation, and theoretical implication. Different titles, settings, terminology, or added moderators do not automatically create independent ideas. Identify alternatives within the same family and retain the strongest or most informative version, without deleting the comparison history.

This adapts the distinction between semantic families and surface variation in Patel and colleagues.[^patel] Diversity is a property of the candidate set, not an extra reward that rescues a weak individual idea. Neither a family quota nor an arbitrary diversity multiplier is imposed.

### 13.2 Compare like with like

Use the same rubric version, contribution route, score base, stage, and development budget for a numerical ranking. Give candidates comparable descriptions and inspection effort. Record any asymmetry, such as one idea having an inspected pilot and another only a one-paragraph sketch.

For mixed routes, compare the concrete contribution, information that could be gained, commitment required, and decision branches in prose. Do not rank a discovery opportunity below an explanatory project solely because its theory score is pending. Do not reward it with the score of a hypothetical future theory either.

An EXPLAIN idea can outrank another intellectually and still be a worse immediate investment because it requires an unavailable design. Report both conclusions. Conversely, a convenient project is not the stronger idea merely because it is executable.

### 13.3 Separate generation, checking, and judgment

When using an assistant or a research team, keep candidate generation, source and premise checking, and final evaluation as distinguishable tasks. Record who performed them and what material each saw. Where possible, have a fresh evaluator inspect standardized briefs without seeing the generator, prior scores, or favored ranking.

A second response from the same model is not independent human validation. Agreement among models is not proof of correctness. Use exact sources to support novelty and premise judgments, and inspect the reasoning behind disagreement rather than merely averaging the numbers.

### 13.4 Report ranking stability

Show the main reason one candidate is preferred and the uncertainty that could reverse that preference. A small numerical lead does not establish a meaningful difference. Test plausible adjacent ratings and development assumptions. When rankings reverse, report a provisional ordering or a tie and identify the action that could resolve it.

Select the next investment, not just the most flattering description of a completed future paper. Retain promising families that need a bounded check; do not turn every initial screen into a full empirical audit.

## 14. Worked examples

All examples are hypothetical protocol applications or arithmetic checks. They are not ratings of the user's projects, published benchmark papers, or editorial outcomes. Numerical inputs are assumed for illustration; actual ratings require the records and comparisons specified above.

### 14.1 An articulated explanation with no study yet

An idea provides a precise question, inspected predecessors, a nontrivial explanation, defined constructs, scope, and a serious rival. Suppose its seven assessed theory ratings are all 7. Then `I_raw = 70`, and it meets the strong-idea profile if no premise or audience block remains.

No study has been specified. Report `D_raw = PENDING` and `P_raw = PENDING`. The next action is to identify the evidence and setting needed. Do not raise the idea score because a large dataset is available, and do not report a strong-project endorsement from the idea score alone.

### 14.2 A worthwhile discovery proposal without an explanation

Several credible records suggest that organizations receiving similar formal evaluations make very different operational changes. The researcher proposes to establish the pattern and learn how the difference arises, but has not yet developed an explanation.

Record the basis for the observation, its uncertain scope, why resolving it matters, existing descriptions, and a bounded plan to compare cases. Use the discovery route assessment. The future explanatory block is `PENDING`, not zero, and an excellent interview or archival plan is not a mechanism score.

A pilot may be justified if it could establish whether the contrast is real and reveal useful distinctions. Favorable results do not automatically justify an exceptional theory rating; assess the eventual explanation when it exists. An absent or misdescribed pattern should change the next question rather than be concealed.

### 14.3 A direct test with no new moderator

An influential explanation predicts that an organizational practice improves a consequential outcome, but the evidence underlying that claim leaves a central comparison unresolved. A new study can make that comparison under conditions the theory actually specifies.

Use TEST. Inspect the original claim, its evidence, the implementation of the test, and what supporting, conflicting, negligible, or imprecise findings would mean. A direct replication of the claim is not a defect. Do not force the researcher to invent a moderator to pass a novelty test. If the result later changes the explanation rather than only confidence in it, identify that additional contribution and assess it separately.

### 14.4 Compensation and rounding do not bypass gates

Suppose `I_uncapped = 80`, `D_raw = 30`, and a central design mismatch exists. The uncapped project average is 55; the reported `P_raw` is capped at 39 and the mismatch blocks submission readiness. The idea can remain separately strong.

Suppose an idea has `I_raw = 72` but dimension 3 is 6. It does not meet the strong-idea profile because the theoretical-advance threshold is 7. That does not make it a poor or irreparable idea: dimension 3 is still above the ordinary reference. State the missed profile rather than using a fatal-flaw label.

Suppose an unrounded idea score is 69.6. Display 70, but do not mark the 70-point gate as passed. Show the unrounded value when needed to explain the decision.

### 14.5 A repair with an unchanged failure branch

Assume comparable EXPLAIN project scores after applicable caps and the same budget `B`.

| Project/action | Current `S0` | `S_plus` | `S_minus` | `p` | `V(B)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: specified, feasible repair | 54 | 70 | 54 | 0.80 | 66.8 |
| B: no identified improvement within the budget | 58 | 58 | 58 | Not needed | 58.0 |

A is weaker now but has the higher expected post-step strength under these assumptions. Its current score remains 54. The calculation does not grant an advance repair. For `p` between 0.60 and 0.95, A's index ranges from 63.6 to 69.2 under the fixed branch scores.

Now suppose the check may reveal a problem that leaves an explicitly described, viable same-route version at 20 rather than 54. Then at `p = 0.80`, A's index is 60, and it exceeds B only when:

```text
p > (58-20)/(70-20) = 0.76
```

At `p = 0.60`, its index is 50. This is a different decision, not an innocuous change in the probability. The viable 20-point branch must actually be described; it cannot stand for an undefined rescued project.

### 14.6 A decisive check when the original version might end

A project depends on an empirical premise that has not been established. An inexpensive records check could support it, refute it, or remain inconclusive. Record what each outcome would change before committing to the full study.

If refutation would end the explanatory version and no comparable alternative is specified, leave that branch as `STOP` and withhold a numerical `V(B)`. The check can still be the correct next action because it prevents a larger unsupported commitment. Do not avoid the check to preserve the current score, or assign a fictitious fallback score to complete the formula.

### 14.7 Relevance is not demonstrated impact

A study could improve how a regulator or manager distinguishes an announcement from an operational result. That supplies a possible use. A short diagnostic guide and discussion with users supply a proposed route to use. Evidence that they read it establishes attention; evidence that it informed a decision establishes use. A beneficial outcome requires further evidence.

Do not award a higher theoretical score for planning a guide, and do not count the same proposed use as both achieved impact and extra research quality. Where the recommendation extends beyond tested evidence, label it as a proposed implication.

## 15. Calibration and reliability

### 15.1 Freeze the version before the pilot

Use a predefined spread of 10–15 accepted papers from relevant conversations as an anchor-and-reliability pilot. Do not select only prize winners, famous authors, or examples that fit the evaluator's preferred style. Preserve the material inspected and comparable-stage briefs. Include live ideas as development cases, but do not treat their proposed outcomes as known.

Use at least two independent human assessments where possible and record expertise and source coverage. When only model-based assessments are available, label that limit. Test clarity and consistency; do not call repeated model agreement human validation.

For articulated theory, assess the argument before showing results or author prestige where practical. For inductive work, do not delete the observations necessary to understand the completed contribution in the name of blinding. Evaluate the original proposal separately when it is actually available; never reconstruct it as though the eventual finding had been known.

### 15.2 What the pilot should test

Check whether different readers identify the same contribution, binding weakness, and next action; whether rating anchors are distinguishable; where dimensions are double-counted; and whether plausible adjacent ratings or modest weight changes reverse decisions. Test the route distinctions with genuine discovery and direct-testing cases.

An improvement in the score alone is not evidence that the protocol improved the idea. Obtain judgments of standardized before-and-after briefs with scores and favored versions hidden where possible. Ask whether the contribution became clearer, better supported, more distinct from prior work, or more consequential. Patel and colleagues' distinction between selection on a metric and additional blinded evaluation motivates this safeguard by analogy.[^patel]

Do not use the small pilot to fit ten weights, force a normal distribution, estimate rare upper tails, or forecast editorial outcomes. Keep all version changes explicit. Reassess old examples under the new version before interpreting score changes as project improvement.

### 15.3 Expand validation by purpose

For comparative quality, expand the reference sample using a stated selection rule and inspect the resulting profiles and disagreements. Validate within contribution routes before making claims about cross-route numerical comparability. Do not use the absence of a universal total as an excuse to exclude valuable non-explanatory work.

For the requested editorial targets, different evidence is needed:

| Target | Evidence needed to investigate it |
|---|---|
| Desk review | Comparable-stage rejected and externally reviewed submissions. |
| One revision round | Initial submissions and revision histories, not just final accepted papers. |
| Editorial advocacy | Direct or independently elicited informed editorial judgments. |
| Decade-level influence | Historical influence evidence, or an explicitly aspirational designation. |

These outcomes need not form one reliable ladder. Fit, disagreement, and additional evidential requirements can differ even among strong papers. Retain `UNCALIBRATED` until an appropriate mapping is supported. Citation or online-attention rankings do not calibrate all of these outcomes.[^scholarlyimpact]

### 16.3 Final assessment check

Before delivering the evaluation, confirm that the actual project has been assessed rather than an imagined better one; sources and inspection limits are explicit; strengths and objections are concrete; missing evidence is distinguished from contradiction; appropriate hard stops and stage rules are applied; numerical claims are reproducible; and the next action addresses the binding issue.

Check that the assessment does not demand an unpromised mechanism, a new moderator merely for novelty, a stakeholder checklist, or direct observation of every theoretical step. Confirm that essential support is still required for the actual claim. Make the final recommendation consistent with the scores and reasons, not reassuring in one place and fatalistic in another.

The final summary should identify the contribution, the insight worth preserving, the binding objection, what the available evidence supports, and the next action. A longer list of criticisms is not a more rigorous assessment.

### 17.2 Do not flatten disagreements between sources

Van de Ven (1989) helps judge an explanation; Hambrick challenges making a new explanation compulsory for every valuable study. V4 handles that tension through contribution routing, not by reducing explanatory standards.

Zuckerman favors focus, while Colquitt and George warn against insufficient scope. V4 asks for the smallest argument that fully answers the question, including necessary competing explanations and evidence.

Van de Ven (2016) emphasizes stakeholder perspectives, while Bansal and colleagues defend critical distance. V4 uses engagement to understand a problem, not to grant users a veto over research value. The discussion of closed stakeholder minds in Van de Ven is not adopted as a scientific rejection rule.

Aguinis and colleagues operationalize external impact through online visibility; Wickert and colleagues discuss several forms of influence on understanding and action. V4 does not equate attention with beneficial consequences. Similarly, normative goals and recommendations in the impact essays are not treated as empirical findings that particular interventions work.

The sources include specific perspectives, not a unanimous doctrine. The rules above state V4's synthesis. They should be tested in use rather than attributed wholesale to any one author.
